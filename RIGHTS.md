# Asset rights

The licence question is answered, and the answer is restrictive.

## The terms

GDQuest's *Your First 2D Game With Godot 4* pack splits its licensing:

- **Code — MIT.** Permissive. Commercially fine. Not a problem.
- **Game assets — CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.** This is the constraint.

CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 means three things at once:

- **BY** — attribution required, in a specific form, visible to players.
- **NC** — **NonCommercial. The assets may not be used commercially, in their
  original *or modified* form.**
- **SA** — ShareAlike. Any derivative must be re-released under the same
  licence.

## What this means for Steam

**Selling Slimer is not permitted** while any pack art remains in it. Not at
any price. The NC clause covers modified assets explicitly, so the recoloured
enemies — which are hue rotations of GDQuest's slime — are as restricted as the
originals.

**Releasing it free is workable**, and the compliance work is already done
(below). Steam still charges the $100 Direct fee for a free game; paying a
platform fee is not the same as selling the work, so that does not by itself
make the use commercial. If you ever want to add paid DLC, a paid version, or
run ads against it, that changes.

**If you want to sell it, the pack art has to go.** That is a bounded job, not
a rewrite — see below.

I am not a lawyer, and CC's NonCommercial definition is fact-specific at the
edges. If real money is going to depend on the answer, ask GDQuest directly
(<https://school.gdquest.com/about-us/contact>) — they may well grant a
commercial licence, and it costs one email to find out.

## Compliance already in place

Everything CC BY-NC-SA asks for, for the current free-distribution state:

- **`game/LICENSE-ASSETS.md`** — the required LICENSE file, with the full
  attribution block, the asset name, links to the original, to GDQuest and to
  the licence, and a per-file table of exactly what was changed.
- **In-game credits screen** — the licence names the credits screen as the
  correct place for a game. It carries the attribution, the links, the
  statement that changes were made, and a line saying the game may be shared
  but not sold.
- **ShareAlike honoured** — the modified assets are re-released under
  CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- **Surface reduced** — pack files the game never actually used have been
  deleted rather than shipped: the Boo legs and feet, `circle_32`, the slime
  hurt frames, the smoke-explosion shader, and 12 of the 15 potion files.

Ten files. Everything else in the game is generated.

| File | Used for | Changed |

| `boo_body.png`, `boo_face.png` | Player character | Yes |
| `pistol.png` | The gun | No |

| `muzzle_flash.png` | Muzzle flash | No |
| `impact_circle.png` | Impact puff | Tinted |
| `pine_tree.png` | One of four tree types | No |
| `ground_shadow.png` | Actor shadows | No |

**Original and unrestricted:** all five boss sprites, every other environment
prop, all four ground tiles, every UI icon, coins, essence, particles, rings,
the app icons, and all 46 sound effects — generated by `tools/gen_art.py` and
`tools/gen_sfx.py`.

## If you decide to sell it

Six things need replacing: a slime body, a player, a pistol and its bullet,
one tree, a shadow, and three potion bottles. The generator already produces 88
sprites in this exact flat-vector style, so these are a known quantity rather
than an unknown. Once they are gone, the art is entirely yours and the NC
clause no longer applies to anything in the build.

Say the word and I will write them.

## Two other things to check before release

- **The name "Slimer"** is a Ghostbusters character. Fine for a free hobby
  project; worth a trademark search and a look at the Steam catalogue before
  putting it on a store page.
- **Your music** — if any of it used sample packs or AI tools, check those
  terms too. Sample libraries commonly restrict redistributing unaltered loops.
