What it is

A top-down roguelike that grows the crowd, not the health bars

You are dropped into a procedurally generated forest with one pistol and exactly two abilities. Waves of coloured slimes come for you; every fifth wave sends a boss, every twentieth a much larger one, and each boss is followed by a shop where the coins you picked up buy upgrades you cannot all afford. Die and the money and the upgrades go with you — only Essence survives, and that is what unlocks new options for the next run. There is no final wave. The run ends when you do.

The loop

Wave, boss, shop, repeat — forever

A mini-boss lands on every fifth wave and a major boss on every twentieth. Each shop is a fork: six offers, priced so you can typically afford two. Bosses you have already beaten come back with an extra phase.

What death takes

Coins, every gun upgrade and every stat boost from the run. The forest is thrown away and reseeded.

What death leaves

Essence: wave × 2 + minis × 12 + majors × 60. It buys the nine locked abilities, five extra shop entries and eight small starting passives.

Why that matters

The permanent unlocks are weighted towards variety, not power. Run twenty should have more possible shapes than run one — not be easier.

The forest

Generated fresh every run, and proven playable before you see it

A 4800 × 3700 arena, built from a new seed each time. Clearings are scattered and pushed apart, joined by paths along a minimum spanning tree plus three extra edges — so the map has loops to circle-strafe rather than dead ends to get cornered in. Then it is rasterised into a walkable grid and flood-filled to prove every clearing can actually be reached. If it cannot, the generator widens the chokepoints and tries again.

1 — scatter clearings

One rule, everywhere

Brown and grey stop you. Green does not. Trees, rocks, logs and stumps block. Grass, bushes and ferns are cover you run straight through. You never have to test a bush to find out.

Clearance is baked in

Blockers are inflated by a 46 px actor clearance before rasterising, so an open cell already means a slime fits. It is why bosses with a 190 px radius do not wedge between two trees and strand the run.

200 seeds, no retries

The test suite generates 200 forests and asserts every clearing, the shop point and every loot point is reachable. All 200 pass on the first attempt, about 74 ms each.

The roster

Six colours, and colour is a promise

Every colour keeps one behaviour and one stat block, declared together in a single table entry so they cannot drift apart. Learn a colour once and it means the same thing at wave 40. Each cell below is running that colour's real steering rule against a dummy player.

Distances above are the game's own. Actors are drawn at double size so they read at card scale.

Elites, from wave 13

A crowned slime is an elite: ×2.6 health, ×1.5 damage, ×3 money, ×1.32 size — and ×2.1 threat, so an elite-heavy wave contains fewer bodies rather than simply more danger. One of three modifiers is rolled on top.

Elite chance is 6% at wave 13, rising 1.2 points a wave to a hard cap of 30% at wave 33.

Shielded

A shield pool worth 45% of max health absorbs everything before health is touched.

Enraged

×1.5 speed and ×1.25 contact damage — and below half health the speed jumps to ×1.85.

Splitter

On death it breaks into two of its own colour at half health, so killing it is only most of the job.

Bosses

Five archetypes, nine attacks, and every one of them telegraphed

Three mini-bosses rotate through the wave-5 slots and two majors hold waves 20 and 40. All five draw from the same nine attacks — slam, radial, aimed volley, charge, summon, spiral, rain, shockwave and blink radial — and add a phase every time they come back around.

Phase changes are a beat, not a number

Crossing a health threshold interrupts whatever the boss was doing, fires 0.18 s of hit-stop, shakes the screen, pulses a ring, throws 30 particles and prints the phase — then holds a 0.9 second invulnerable pause so you can actually register that the fight just changed.

Minis start at two phases and majors at three. Every repeat visit adds one, up to the archetype's full attack list.

Nothing lands without warning

Every heavy attack paints its danger on the ground first, and the marker fills from the centre outward to end exactly at the radius that will be hit. Later phases shorten the warning — never below 0.42 seconds.

The ring always has a seam

The radial burst never closes fully. A perfect ring is a damage tax you pay; a ring with a gap is a decision you get to make. The gap moves every time, so it is something to look for rather than something to memorise.

The line comes before the charge

A charging boss draws its path before it moves. Safe ground is never ambiguous — you are being asked to step aside, not to guess.

Difficulty

A threat budget, not a health-bar multiplier

Each wave gets a budget of 4 + 2.1w + 0.075w² threat and spends it on whatever the roster allows — a green slime costs 1.0, a brute 3.0, an elite 2.1 times its base. Meanwhile what any individual enemy gets is hard capped: health stops at ×3.2, damage at ×2.4, speed at ×1.45. A wave-40 enemy is a few times tougher than a wave-1 one, not a thousand times. The difficulty is the crowd and the combination.

Hover or drag across the chart to read any wave. Gold bands are mini-boss waves, red are majors.

Spawn rate

min(1.1 + 0.18w, 7.5) threat per second, so late waves arrive faster as well as heavier.

Opening cluster

Every wave opens with 35% of its budget at once, so it starts as an event rather than an ambient drip.

Spawn placement

620–1250 px away, on open ground, preferably off-screen. Enemies that spawn at the far limit spend ten seconds walking, which is not a fight.

Finisher mode

Once the budget is spent and four or fewer remain, every survivor commits. Hoarders stop fleeing, spitters stop kiting.

Abilities

Eleven exist. You carry two.

That limit is the design. Two slots force a choice between getting out, doing damage, controlling the field and staying alive — and you can only change your mind at a shop. Dash and Grenade are free from the start; the other nine are bought once, permanently, with Essence.

Three potions drop while you fight. Red restores 32% health, blue gives +45% move speed for 9 seconds, purple refills your ability charges and clears both cooldowns. A potion you cannot use dims, stops expiring and waits — and the magnet ignores it, so a red potion at full health is never wasted on you by accident.

Forest Rest

Six offers, and you cannot buy everything

There is one gun for the whole run and no weapon inventory. Purchases edit the numbers that gun reads, so an upgrade applies on the very next shot. Six offers are drawn from a pool of 22, priced so you can typically afford two — and every repeat purchase of the same upgrade costs 55% of its base price more than the last, so stacking one stat has a real cost against branching out.

The offer list reads your situation. Anything you have already stacked shows up less often. Field Poultice is weighted almost to zero at full health and becomes one of the likeliest offers when you are nearly dead — so the shop is responding to the run you are actually having.

Sound

Four tracks, nine cues, one continuous take

Two decks crossfade over 1.4 seconds. When the track underneath is the same one, the incoming deck inherits the playhead — so escalating in the middle of a wave feels like the music leaning in, not restarting. The combat theme is pitched down and driven harder for bosses rather than swapped out.

Every music cue and what triggers it. Press play to hear the underlying track — nothing plays until you ask it to.
CueTrackRateDriveWhen

46 synthesised effects

Every sound effect is generated by script, not sampled — including the percussion layers, written to 100 BPM so they sit on the music.

A fixed 24-voice ring

A hectic wave can never spawn unbounded audio players. Voices are reused round-robin, preferring finished ones, with ±6% pitch jitter.

Per-sound throttling

A kill dropping five coins used to play five copies of the same blip on top of each other. Now it does not.

65 MB · universal

Runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel. It is ad-hoc signed but not notarised, so macOS quarantines the download and says “Slimer is damaged and can’t be opened”. It is not damaged — that is the message macOS uses for anything it cannot verify. Right-click → Open does not clear it. Move the app where you want it, then run this once:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Slimer.app
